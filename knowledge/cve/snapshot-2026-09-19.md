## Source says: vulnerability and publication

The [rules §4.1](https://www.cve.org/Resources/Roles/Cnas/CNA_Rules_v4.1.0.pdf) and [CVE Program Glossary](https://www.cve.org/ResourcesSupport/Glossary) describe a technical cybersecurity vulnerability as exploitable weakness(es) in a product causing negative confidentiality, integrity, or availability impact, or conditions/behavior that violate an explicit or implicit security policy. Conditions without security impact generally should not be determined vulnerabilities (§4.1.2). Supplier determinations receive weight, but the CNA exercises judgment (§4.1, §4.4).

Under [§4.2.2–4.2.7](https://www.cve.org/Resources/Roles/Cnas/CNA_Rules_v4.1.0.pdf), a CNA should assign when it has reasonable evidence of a vulnerability, the issue is or is expected to be publicly disclosed, and it has appropriate scope. An assigned ID must lead to public disclosure or be rejected. IDs refer to vulnerabilities, not fixes; a fix need not exist first. A product that is not and was never publicly available generally should not receive an ID (§4.2.10). End-of-life status alone is not a vulnerability (§4.1.13, §4.2.17.7), but a vulnerability in EOL software may still receive an ID subject to EOL procedures (§4.2.17). A supported and EOL version sharing the same vulnerability do not receive separate IDs solely for that reason (§4.2.17.8).

**Research interpretation:** a human-validated WordPress issue with demonstrated security impact may appear consistent with CVE vulnerability concepts even when a bounty excludes it. That is a preliminary assessment, not a binding CNA determination or an assigned CVE. Software closed to new downloads may still have been publicly available; check its history and the applicable CNA's handling rather than equating closure with §4.2.10.

## Source says: scope and first opportunity

The [rules §3.1 and §4.2.1](https://www.cve.org/Resources/Roles/Cnas/CNA_Rules_v4.1.0.pdf) require published CNA scope definitions and the most appropriate CNA's first opportunity to assign; that is often, but not invariably, the supplier. Research/coordinator CNAs may cover issues they research or coordinate when a more specific CNA does not (§3.1.8). Overlapping CNAs coordinate, with Root-level resolution when needed (§4.2.16). An upstream supplier CNA receives first refusal for its dependency when the downstream supplier CNA becomes aware (§4.2.16.6). Avoid parallel CVE requests that could create duplicates (§4.2.19–4.2.20).

The official [Wordfence partner listing](https://www.cve.org/PartnerInformation/ListofPartners/partner/Wordfence) states Wordfence's CNA scope as WordPress plugins, themes, and core vulnerabilities discovered by or reported to the Wordfence/Defiant team. **Research interpretation:** this identifies a possible coordinator, not automatic ownership of every WordPress issue. For a specific finding, identify the supplier/project and check the current [List of Partners](https://www.cve.org/PartnerInformation/ListofPartners), supplier CNA scope, Wordfence scope, and disclosure policies. Do not promise Wordfence assignment merely because a report goes there; current [Wordfence program texts](../bounty/wordfence/snapshot-2026-09-19.md#unresolved-official-source-conflicts) also disagree about out-of-scope CVE handling.

## Source says: vulnerability abstraction

The [rules §4.1.10–4.1.12 and §4.2.8–4.2.15](https://www.cve.org/Resources/Roles/Cnas/CNA_Rules_v4.1.0.pdf) ask whether issues are independently fixable; interdependent conditions normally form one vulnerability. Separate independently fixable vulnerabilities generally receive different IDs. If independence is uncertain, the rules recommend one ID. The same vulnerable code shared by multiple products must not receive multiple IDs solely for those products. Updating a vulnerable dependency is not itself a new vulnerability. Residual insecurity after an incomplete fix can be handled as a new issue or under the old ID by CNA judgment. A chain of effects, several endpoints, the same CWE, and several patches are each insufficient on their own to decide CVE count.

**Research interpretation:** describe the root weakness, affected components and versions, whether one fix can address each manifestation independently, dependency/upstream origin, and whether a supposed bypass is residual insecurity. Keep the [finding's attack chain](../../docs/findings.md) separate from any proposed CVE grouping. Only a CNA/CVE record establishes an actual ID and its [published or rejected state](https://www.cve.org/Resources/Roles/Cnas/CNA_Rules_v4.1.0.pdf).

## Revalidation and limits

Before recommending a CNA or describing likely assignment, recheck the operative rules version, current partner scopes and product history, public disclosure plan, duplicates/records, and unresolved Wordfence policy. Record what the source says versus the researcher's interpretation in the [CVE assessment](../../templates/cve-relevance-assessment.md). CVSS severity may be considered separately after human review; it proves neither vulnerability existence nor bounty scope nor CVE assignment. Historical vulnerability records are prior evidence, not assignment rules.
